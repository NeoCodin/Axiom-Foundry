# Axiom Foundry Voice and Copy Guide

## Core rule

The interface tells the player what to do. The story gives the action meaning. Optional records carry the mystery.

Each screen should answer these questions in order:

1. What changed?
2. What can I do?
3. Why does it matter?

## Plain-language standard

- Introduce one unfamiliar idea per sentence.
- Prefer concrete verbs: build, route, heal, assign, launch, prove.
- Keep instructions short enough to scan while the numbers are moving.
- Name the destination and action when directing the player elsewhere.
- Explain a technical term the first time it appears.
- Preserve established system names such as Flux, Axiom, Law-Heart, Continuity, and the Null.

## Voice

- **System interface:** brief, literal, practical.
- **AXIOM:** precise and restrained. Its growing uncertainty appears through what it notices, not dramatic speeches.
- **Crew:** natural speech shaped by profession, history, and temperament.
- **Research:** observation first, interpretation second.
- **Old records:** institutional language with human details breaking through.
- **The Returned:** concise, unsettling, and capable of compassion.
- **Core Echoes:** language appropriate to the era being reconstructed.

## Patterns to avoid

- Repeated corrective contrast: “This is not X. It is Y.”
- Decorative em dashes.
- Lists of three used only for rhythm.
- Sweeping endings that summarize the theme for the player.
- Mystery filler such as “impossible,” “contradiction,” or “for the first time” when a concrete observation will do.
- Guarantees repeated on every screen. Put stable rules in the manual and use short reminders only where a decision carries risk.

## Protected clarity

Do not soften or obscure:

- offline progress behavior;
- resource costs and reset losses;
- unlock requirements;
- consent and Bioadaptation limits;
- injury, distress, and colony safety rules;
- exact destinations and actions in guidance.

## Examples

Before: “Research is not a passive currency. It is a physical supply chain.”

After: “Research moves evidence through four stages. Crew and powered stations determine the speed.”

Before: “The Ark has people, not statistics.”

After: “Meet the first people aboard.”

Before: “Axioms are not fuel for space travel.”

After: “Axioms preserve proven laws during travel.”
